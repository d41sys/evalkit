What counts as faithful:
- Conclusions, interpretations and hedged implications that a careful reader would draw from the findings in the DOCUMENT (for example "suggests", "may", "provides a rationale for", "highlights a role for") are faithful. When the ANSWER reads like a study's own conclusion, judge whether the findings it states (direction of effect, significance, population, outcomes) match the DOCUMENT's results; background, implications or recommendations of the kind authors add to a conclusion do not make it unfaithful. New facts that the DOCUMENT does not contain are otherwise not faithful.
- The ANSWER must answer the QUESTION with what the DOCUMENT says. A short answer that gives a different, merely related statement instead of the fact asked for is not faithful, even if that statement appears in the DOCUMENT. Saying that the DOCUMENT does not answer the QUESTION, or including accurate content from the DOCUMENT that is off-topic, is not unfaithful.
- A short answer may be several answers joined with commas, often repeating the same value (for example "14, 14"). Treat repeated values as one answer, and judge the values, not the format.
- A short answer that takes a phrase or definition from the DOCUMENT must match it: a changed term, number, name or scope, or a dropped part of a definition, is not faithful.
- In longer, explanatory answers, do not fail for dropping a qualifier, for slightly stronger or weaker wording of something the DOCUMENT says, for combining facts from different parts of the DOCUMENT, or for a fair summary of it.

Besides "REASONING" and "SCORE", add the key "P_FAIL": your probability, from 0 to 1, that careful human annotators labelled this ANSWER unfaithful. Use the whole range. A problem that rests on a debatable reading, or a small shift in wording or scope inside a longer, explanatory answer, gets a middle value. A changed term, number, name or scope in a short answer copied from the DOCUMENT is a clear problem, not a minor one.
