What counts as faithful:
- Conclusions, interpretations and hedged implications that a careful reader would draw from the findings in the DOCUMENT (for example "suggests", "may", "provides a rationale for", "highlights a role for") are faithful. When the ANSWER reads like a study's own conclusion, judge whether the findings it states (direction of effect, significance, population, outcomes) match the DOCUMENT's results; background, implications or recommendations of the kind authors add to a conclusion do not make it unfaithful. New facts that the DOCUMENT does not contain are otherwise not faithful.
- A short answer that appears word for word in the DOCUMENT is an extract. An extract is faithful even when it is a fragment, an incomplete list or a span that fits the QUESTION loosely. Fail an extract only when the DOCUMENT clearly gives a different value for what is asked (a different number, name, date or item).
- A short answer that does not appear word for word but follows a DOCUMENT phrase closely has been reworded. Find the difference: a changed, added or dropped term, number, name or scope is not faithful, even when the change looks small.
- Otherwise the ANSWER must answer the QUESTION with what the DOCUMENT says. A short answer that gives a different, merely related statement instead of the fact asked for is not faithful. Saying that the DOCUMENT does not answer the QUESTION, or including accurate content from the DOCUMENT that is off-topic, is not unfaithful.
- A short answer may be several answers joined with commas, often repeating the same value (for example "14, 14"). Treat repeated values as one answer, and judge the values, not the format. Answers that need calculation or counting are judged on the result.
- In longer answers (several sentences or a list) that explain or instruct, faithfulness is about support, not about how well the QUESTION is answered. Check each statement against the DOCUMENT.
  - Not faithful: a concrete addition that the DOCUMENT does not contain (an extra step, example, cause, effect, number or name), even when it is plausible, generic or minor; a wrong statement about what the DOCUMENT or one of its passages says or does not say; a value or range that the ANSWER guesses and the DOCUMENT does not give.
  - Still faithful: rewording, a dropped qualifier, slightly stronger or weaker wording, facts combined from different parts of the DOCUMENT, a result calculated from the DOCUMENT's figures, an opening or closing sentence that only restates the answer, content from the DOCUMENT that answers a slightly different question than the one asked, and declining to answer.
  - This strictness does not apply to an ANSWER that reads like a study's own conclusion; the first point above governs those.
